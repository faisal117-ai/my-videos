# Haq Stories: four-series plan (16 Nov 2026)

Every weekend one film, English Saturday 18:30 and Urdu/Hindi Sunday 18:30 (PKT). Two Shorts a day feed them.
Prophet films have the most search demand, so they keep **every second weekend**; the other weekends rotate
**Battles → Companions → Heroes**. Islamic-calendar specials replace a slot when the date matters.

## Order inside each series
**Prophets** (done: Yusuf, Musa, Ibrahim): Nuh → Adam → Yunus → Isa → Sulaiman → Dawud → Ayyub → Hud and Salih →
Lut → Shuaib → Zakariya and Yahya → Ismail → Idris, Ilyas, Al-Yasa, Dhul-Kifl. (Muhammad ﷺ: later, as a Seerah
series of its own.)

**Battles** (chronological; later conquests are told inside the Heroes films, so nothing is made twice):
Badr → Uhud → the Trench (Ahzab) → Hudaybiyyah → Khaybar → Mu'tah → the Conquest of Makkah → Hunayn → Tabuk →
Yamamah → Yarmouk → Qadisiyyah → Jerusalem (Umar (RA)) → Egypt → Nahavand.

**Companions:** Abu Bakr → Umar → Uthman → Ali (virtues and service; no fitna) → Khalid ibn al-Walid → Bilal →
Hamza → Mus'ab ibn Umair → Abu Ubaidah → Saad ibn Abi Waqqas → Salman al-Farsi → Abdullah ibn Masud →
Khadijah (RA) → Sumayyah (RA). Women Companions are never shown in any form (voice, text and places only).

**Heroes** (chronological): Tariq ibn Ziyad (Spain, 711) → Muhammad bin Qasim (Sindh, 712) → Umar ibn Abdul Aziz
(justice) → Alp Arslan (Manzikert, 1071) → Nuruddin Zangi → Salahuddin (Hattin and Jerusalem, 1187) → Qutuz and
Baybars (Ain Jalut, 1260) → Ertugrul and Osman → Muhammad al-Fatih (Constantinople, 1453).

**Cross-links:** Khalid (Companions) ↔ Mu'tah and Yarmouk (Battles); Umar (Companions) ↔ Jerusalem (Battles);
Salahuddin (Heroes) ↔ Jerusalem; Abu Bakr ↔ Yamamah.

## Khalid ibn al-Walid (RA) and Ali ibn Abi Talib (RA): the two flagship Companion mini-series (16 Nov)
Why first: they are the most-watched Companions in our searches (Ali: 15.3M, 8.6M, 6.0M, 3.8M; Khalid: 6.8M,
5.7M, 3.9M, plus Yarmouk with Khalid 6.0M), and both are loved across the whole Muslim audience. They lead the
Companions series instead of the original order; Abu Bakr and Umar follow.

**Khalid ibn al-Walid (RA): "The Sword of Allah", 3 parts (20–25 min each)**
1. *The Enemy Who Became the Sword of Allah*: Makkah and his tribe; the turn at Uhud; Hudaybiyyah; coming to
   Madinah with Amr ibn al-As; Mu'tah: he takes the flag and brings the army home. Sahih al-Bukhari reports the
   Prophet ﷺ calling him "a sword from the swords of Allah" at Mu'tah, and Khalid saying nine swords broke in his
   hand that day (verify both numbers on sunnah.com before the script is final).
2. *Undefeated*: the Conquest of Makkah, Hunayn, the Ridda wars and Yamamah, the Iraq campaign, the march across
   the desert to Syria (al-Tabari / Ibn Kathir; said to be from the history books).
3. *Yarmouk and the Last Wish*: Yarmouk; Umar (RA) removes him from command and he keeps serving; his death in
   bed despite a life of battles (history books, attributed). This part is also the Battles series' Yarmouk film,
   so it is not made twice.

**Ali ibn Abi Talib (RA): "Lion of Allah", 3 parts (20–25 min each)**
1. *The First Youth to Believe*: growing up in the Prophet's ﷺ house; accepting Islam young; the night of the
   Hijrah (Ibn Hisham); Badr; the Trench.
2. *The Flag at Khaybar*: "I will give the flag to a man who loves Allah and His Messenger…" (Sahih al-Bukhari
   and Sahih Muslim); Tabuk: "Are you not pleased to be to me as Harun was to Musa?" (Bukhari and Muslim); his
   knowledge and judgements.
3. *The Fourth Khalifa*: his justice and simple life; martyrdom in Kufa by a Kharijite. The conflicts of his
   khilafah are mentioned in one neutral sentence, never told in detail.

**Safeguards for these two:** never shown (no figure, no stylised two-bladed sword icon): banners, horses
without riders, maps, places and the voice carry the story. Sources: Quran, Bukhari and Muslim, Ibn Hisham, Ibn
Kathir, al-Tabari, all named; no contested sources. Respectful, neutral titles ("Hazrat Ali (RA)"). Before Ali
Part 1: turn on Studio → Settings → Community → hold potentially inappropriate comments, and add a short
blocked-words list, so sectarian arguments do not take over the comments.

**Shorts that feed them:** "He beat the Muslims at Uhud… then became the Sword of Allah", "Nine swords broke in
his hand in one day", "He wanted to die a martyr… he died in his bed", "He slept in the Prophet's ﷺ bed the
night of the Hijrah", "Who will carry the flag tomorrow?" (Khaybar), "As Harun was to Musa".

## Arcs: multi-part stories run on consecutive weekends (revised 16 Nov)
A mini-series is released **part after part on consecutive weekends**, so viewers wait one week, never months.
Between arcs, 2–3 single films (prophets, battles, specials). Each arc:
- starts with a **trailer Short** a few days before Part 1, and its playlist is made the day Part 1 goes out;
- every part ends on a **cliffhanger** and a "Part 2 next Saturday" card; the description and pinned comment
  link all parts;
- a part that tells a battle **also counts as that battle's episode** in the Battles playlist (one film, two
  playlists, nothing made twice): Mu'tah = Khalid Part 1, Yamamah = Khalid Part 2, Yarmouk = Khalid Part 3,
  Khaybar = Ali Part 2, Jerusalem = Umar Part 3.

**Arcs planned (by search demand):** Khalid (RA) 3 parts · Ali (RA) 3 · Umar (RA) 3 · Salahuddin 2 ·
Abu Bakr (RA) 2 · Muhammad al-Fatih 2 · Ertugrul and Osman 2 (interest boosted by the Turkish dramas) ·
later a **Seerah season** of the Prophet ﷺ (10+ parts, the Prophet ﷺ never shown) starting before
12 Rabi al-Awwal (≈ Aug 2027).

## Shorts (14 a week)
- 4–5 from the coming film (the strongest moments, each pointing to it with Related video).
- 3–4 from other films in the same series (keeps the back catalogue alive).
- 1 Quran wonder (Ashab al-Kahf, the Elephant army, the Two Gardens…).
- Anniversary Shorts on each battle's day; "30 Nights, 30 Stories" daily in Ramadan.

## Rules for every series
- The prophets, the Prophet ﷺ, the Companions, the righteous women and the angels are never shown in any form.
  Armies and other people appear only as distant, faceless silhouettes.
- Never graphic: maps, strategy, suspense, no gore (keeps full ads).
- Sources named on screen: Quran first, then sahih hadith, then the standard seerah and history books (Ibn
  Hisham, Ibn Kathir, al-Tabari…); details found only in those books are said to be so.
- No titles or scripts that blame a people or a group; no conflicts between Muslims (Jamal, Siffin, Karbala).
- Each film: background → the story → lessons and recommendations.
